Public Benchmarks vs Task-Specific Evals

conceptfreshness: evolvingsources not yet verified

Public benchmarks give comparable numbers across models; task-specific evaluations measure fitness for your actual use. You need both, in that order of decision weight.

Short answer: Use benchmarks to shortlist and task evals to decide. Benchmarks are broad, comparable and public (so contaminable); task evals are narrow, representative and private. Build one early.

What is it?

A benchmark tells you how a model does on someone else's exam. A task eval tells you how it does on your work. The second is the one that predicts whether your product will work.

Also known as: benchmarks vs evals, public benchmarks vs custom evals, should I trust benchmarks, how to choose an LLM for my use case, custom LLM evaluation vs benchmark.

Technical definition

Public benchmarkTask-specific eval
CoverageBroad, genericNarrow, representative
ComparabilityHigh across modelsOnly on your cases
Contamination riskHigher (public)Controllable (private)
Cost to buildNone (existing)You build and maintain
Predicts product qualityWeaklyStrongly
Best useShortlisting, researchSelection, regression testing, monitoring

Entity type: concept · Freshness class: evolving.

When should I use it?

Always build the task eval for anything beyond a throwaway prototype.

Implementation patterns

  • Collect 50–200 real inputs with outcomes or rubrics.
  • Run on 2–4 candidate models.
  • Add cases from production failures.
  • Re-run on every change.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: evaluation harnesses, LLM-as-judge, observability platforms. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Sample real traffic.
  2. Define success criteria.
  3. Label or write rubrics.
  4. Automate runs.
  5. Review failures.

Common failure modes

  • No eval, relying on demos.
  • Eval set not representative.
  • Never updating the eval.

Troubleshooting

  • Cannot decide between two models — Compare on your eval including cost and latency.
