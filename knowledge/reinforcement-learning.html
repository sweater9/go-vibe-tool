Reinforcement Learning

conceptfreshness: stablesources not yet verified

Reinforcement learning trains an agent to choose actions in an environment to maximise cumulative reward through trial and feedback.

Short answer: RL = an agent acts, gets rewards, and learns a policy that maximises long-term reward. It handles sequential decisions (games, robotics, control) but is data-hungry and sensitive to reward design. Preference methods like DPO are not standard RL.

What is it?

Learn by doing: an agent tries actions, receives rewards or penalties, and gradually learns which actions lead to the best long-term outcome — like training a dog with treats, or a program learning chess by playing itself.

Also known as: RL, reinforcement learning basics, policy, reward function, agent environment reward, exploration vs exploitation, deep reinforcement learning, deep RL.

Technical definition

Formalised as a Markov decision process (Markov Decision Processes): states, actions, transition dynamics, rewards and discount factor. Goals: learn a policy π(a|s) maximising expected return. Families: value-based (Q-learning, Deep Q-Networks (DQN) and Q-Learning), policy-gradient and actor-critic (Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)), model-based RL (World Models), offline RL, imitation/inverse RL. Key difficulties: exploration versus exploitation, credit assignment over long horizons, sample efficiency, reward specification (Reward Hacking and Over-Optimization) and stability. Methods that optimise directly on preference pairs without a sampled-reward RL loop, such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), belong to Preference Optimization (Aligning Models from Human or AI Preferences) rather than ordinary RL.

Entity type: concept · Freshness class: stable.

When should I use it?

Use when decisions are sequential and feedback is delayed and a simulator or safe trial-and-error exists.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: Supervised Learning.

Related concepts: Markov Decision Processes, Deep Q-Networks (DQN) and Q-Learning, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning (RLVR and GRPO), RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), Preference Optimization (Aligning Models from Human or AI Preferences), World Models, Imitation Learning (Behaviour Cloning and DAgger).

Implementation patterns

  • Simulate first: train in simulation before real systems.
  • Reward shaping with care and audits.
  • Use proven algorithms (PPO, SAC, DQN) before inventing new ones.
  • Evaluate across many seeds.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: Gymnasium, Stable-Baselines3, RLlib (Ray), TRL. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Define states, actions and reward precisely.
  2. Build or pick a simulator.
  3. Start with a standard algorithm.
  4. Log returns over many seeds.
  5. Test on perturbed environments before deployment.

Common failure modes

  • Reward hacking and unintended behaviour.
  • High variance across random seeds.
  • Poor sample efficiency making real-world training impractical.
  • Training/evaluation environment mismatch.

Troubleshooting

  • Agent finds a loophole — Audit the reward; add constraints and held-out evaluation.
  • Learning unstable — Use established implementations, tune learning rate/advantage normalisation, check seeds.

Contrasted with

  • Supervised Learning — Supervised learns from labelled answers; RL learns from delayed reward after actions.
  • Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) — DPO optimises a policy on preference pairs with a supervised-style loss; it is preference optimisation, not an RL sampling loop.
  • Imitation Learning (Behaviour Cloning and DAgger) — Imitation learns from demonstrations rather than rewards.
