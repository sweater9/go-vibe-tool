Transfer Learning

techniquefreshness: stablesources not yet verified

Transfer learning reuses a model pre-trained on a large task as the starting point for a new, smaller one, saving data and compute.

Short answer: Start from a pre-trained model and adapt it to your task (feature extraction or fine-tuning). It usually beats training from scratch when your data is limited and the domains are related.

What is it?

Instead of teaching a model from zero, borrow one that already learned general skills — recognising shapes, understanding language — and teach it your specific task. It needs far less data and time.

Also known as: pretrained models, pre-trained model fine-tuning, feature extraction, domain adaptation, ImageNet pretrained, catastrophic forgetting, warm start.

Technical definition

Approaches: use the pre-trained model as a frozen feature extractor with a new head; fine-tune all or some layers; parameter-efficient methods (LoRA and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)); continued pre-training on domain data. Yosinski et al. (2014) studied transferability of features by layer. Negative transfer can occur when source and target are very different; catastrophic forgetting can erase general abilities if fine-tuning is aggressive.

Entity type: technique · Freshness class: stable.

When should I use it?

Default approach for images, text and audio when data is limited.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: Neural Networks, Self-Supervised Learning.

Related concepts: What is Fine-Tuning an AI Model?, LoRA and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT), Overfitting and Regularization, Deep Learning, Hugging Face for Builders.

Implementation patterns

  • Freeze backbone, train head first; then unfreeze with a small learning rate.
  • Use domain-specific pre-trained models when available.
  • LoRA for large models.
  • Evaluate for forgetting on a general benchmark.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: Hugging Face Hub, PyTorch / torchvision, timm. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Pick a pre-trained model close to your domain.
  2. Check licence.
  3. Replicate its preprocessing.
  4. Train the head, then unfreeze gradually.
  5. Compare with a from-scratch baseline.

Common failure modes

  • Source domain too different from target.
  • Fine-tuning learning rate too high.
  • Preprocessing mismatch with the pre-trained model.
  • Ignoring licence terms of the pre-trained model.

Troubleshooting

  • Accuracy collapses after fine-tuning — Lower learning rate, freeze early layers, mix in general data.
  • Poor results immediately — Match tokenizer/normalisation/preprocessing to the pre-trained model.

Contrasted with

  • What is Fine-Tuning an AI Model? — Fine-tuning is the most common form of transfer learning for language models.
