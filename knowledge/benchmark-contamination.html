Benchmark Contamination and Data Leakage

conceptfreshness: evolvingsources not yet verified

Contamination happens when benchmark questions or answers appear in a model's training data, inflating scores. It undermines claims of capability and is hard to rule out fully.

Short answer: If a model saw the test during training, its score measures memory, not skill. Mitigations: held-out or freshly created tests, private sets, contamination checks, and evaluating on your own unseen data.

What is it?

Imagine a student who saw the exam questions beforehand. Their high mark says little about what they can do. AI models are trained on enormous amounts of web text, which can include the very tests used to judge them.

Also known as: data contamination, test set leakage, training on the test set, benchmark leakage, contaminated benchmark, LiveCodeBench, canary string, memorization benchmarks.

Technical definition

Contamination can be exact (verbatim overlap), partial (paraphrases, translations) or indirect (derived datasets, solutions on forums). Detection methods include n-gram overlap checks, membership-inference-style probes and performance gaps between original and rephrased/perturbed or newer items; none is conclusive, especially for closed training data. Mitigations include time-split ("live") benchmarks, private test sets, dynamic generation and rephrasing, and canary strings.

Implementation patterns

  • Evaluate on items created after the model's training cutoff.
  • Rephrase or perturb questions and compare scores.
  • Keep your own evaluation set private.
  • Report contamination checks with results.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: n-gram overlap detection, LiveCodeBench-style time-split sets, private held-out sets, perturbed-question variants. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Create a private eval set from real data.
  2. Keep it out of training and prompts.
  3. Add fresh items regularly.
  4. Compare to perturbed variants.
  5. Document provenance.

Common failure modes

  • Assuming absence of evidence of contamination is evidence of absence.
  • Public test sets reused for years.
  • Leaking your private eval into prompts or fine-tuning data.
  • Treating a rephrased item as independent when it is not.

Troubleshooting

  • Suspiciously high score — Compare against perturbed or newer items; examine failures on new variants.
  • Your own eval is leaking — Separate eval data from any training or few-shot examples; restrict access.

Contrasted with

  • Overfitting and Regularization — Overfitting is memorising training data generally; contamination is specifically the test data being in training.
