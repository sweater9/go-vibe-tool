EU AI Act

regulationfreshness: volatilekey claims checked 2026-10-09

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is a risk-based law regulating AI systems placed on or used in the EU market, with prohibited practices, obligations for high-risk systems, transparency duties and rules for general-purpose AI models.

Short answer: A risk-based EU regulation: some AI practices are prohibited; high-risk systems face requirements (risk management, data governance, documentation, human oversight); some systems have transparency duties; general-purpose AI models have their own obligations. Application dates are phased and were amended in 2026 by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 — verify the current consolidated timetable. This is orientation, not legal advice.

What is it?

The EU AI Act sorts AI uses by risk. A few uses are banned outright, risky ones (like AI used in hiring or credit) must meet strict requirements, some need clear disclosure (like chatbots and deepfakes), and the big general-purpose models have their own duties.

Also known as: EU AI Act, AI Act, European AI regulation, Regulation 2024/1689, high-risk AI systems, prohibited AI practices, general purpose AI obligations, GPAI code of practice.

Technical definition

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 entered into force in 2024 with phased application. As commonly summarised: prohibitions and AI-literacy duties applied first; obligations for general-purpose AI models followed; most remaining provisions, including high-risk system requirements in the Act's annexed use cases, apply later, with a longer transition for AI embedded in products covered by existing safety legislation. Roles include provider, deployer, importer and distributor, each with different duties; penalties scale with turnover. The timetable has already been amended: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI), published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and in force from 27 July 2026, moved high-risk obligations for Annex III systems to 2 December 2027 and for AI in products covered by Annex I to 2 August 2028, and added new Article 5 prohibitions that apply from 2 December 2026 (point (ba): AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic intimate or sexually explicit images, video or audio of an identifiable person without explicit consent; point (bb): AI systems that generate or manipulate child sexual abuse material within the meaning of Directive 2011/93/EU; new paragraphs 1a and 1b limit the prohibition to systems whose intended purpose or foreseeable, unsafeguarded outcome is that generation, and to deployers who use a system for that purpose); the original prohibitions have applied since 2 February 2025, and Chapter V (general-purpose AI models) has applied since 2 August 2025 (Article 113 as consolidated on 2026-07-27). Implementing acts, harmonised standards, guidance and corrigenda can still change the practical picture, so exact dates and scope must be checked against the consolidated Official Journal text and current Commission guidance.

When should I use it?

Relevant to anyone offering or deploying AI in or into the EU.

Implementation patterns

  • Inventory AI systems and classify risk category and your role.
  • Gap-assess against the relevant requirements.
  • Maintain technical documentation and logs.
  • Track guidance and standards updates.
  • Get qualified legal advice for decisions.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: conformity assessment processes, harmonised standards, documentation and logging systems. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Identify role and system category.
  2. Read the relevant articles and annexes.
  3. Run a gap assessment.
  4. Plan documentation and oversight.
  5. Revisit as guidance evolves.

Common failure modes

  • Assuming the Act applies only to EU-based companies.
  • Misclassifying risk level.
  • Ignoring deployer duties.
  • Relying on outdated timeline information.

Troubleshooting

  • Unsure whether a use is high-risk — Check the Act's annex categories and official guidance; consult counsel.
  • Timeline questions — Consult the current official timetable; do not rely on secondary summaries.

Contrasted with

  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework — The AI RMF is voluntary guidance; the AI Act is binding law in the EU.
  • ISO/IEC 42001 (AI Management System) — ISO 42001 is a certifiable management-system standard; the Act sets legal obligations.
