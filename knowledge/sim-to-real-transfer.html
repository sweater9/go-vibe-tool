Sim-to-Real Transfer

techniquefreshness: evolvingsources not yet verified

Sim-to-real transfer trains robot policies in simulation and deploys them on real hardware, using techniques such as domain randomisation to bridge the gap between simulated and real physics and sensing.

Short answer: Train cheaply and safely in simulation, then close the reality gap with domain randomisation, system identification, realistic rendering, and real-data fine-tuning. Success depends on how well simulation covers the real conditions you will meet.

What is it?

Robots break things and learn slowly in the real world, so they practise in simulation first. But simulations are never exactly like reality, so the trick is making the practice varied enough — and then fine-tuning on the real robot — so skills carry over.

Also known as: sim2real, sim to real, domain randomization, domain randomisation, reality gap, simulation to reality, robot simulation training, Isaac Sim.

Technical definition

Domain randomisation (Tobin et al., 2017) randomises visual and physical parameters in simulation so that the real world looks like one more variation. Related techniques: system identification and calibration, domain adaptation, residual learning on top of analytic controllers, and teacher–student distillation (privileged information in simulation distilled into a deployable policy). Successes on legged locomotion and dexterous manipulation are widely reported, while contact-rich tasks, deformables and perception remain challenging. Simulators include MuJoCo, Isaac Sim/Lab and PyBullet; capabilities and names change.

Entity type: technique · Freshness class: evolving.

When should I use it?

Standard approach for RL-based robot skills.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: Reinforcement Learning, Embodied AI.

Related concepts: Imitation Learning (Behaviour Cloning and DAgger), Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models, World Models, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), How to Evaluate AI Systems and Outputs.

Implementation patterns

  • Randomise physics (mass, friction, latency) and visuals.
  • Calibrate the simulator to measured robot data.
  • Teacher–student distillation for deployable observations.
  • Staged deployment with safety limits.
  • Real-world fine-tuning.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: MuJoCo, NVIDIA Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab, PyBullet, Gazebo, domain-randomisation tooling. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Calibrate the simulator.
  2. Choose randomisation ranges.
  3. Train and test across the ranges.
  4. Deploy with safety limits.
  5. Collect real data and fine-tune.

Common failure modes

  • Overly narrow randomisation.
  • Unmodelled actuator dynamics and latency.
  • Perception mismatch.
  • Policies exploiting simulator quirks.
  • Skipping safety constraints on first hardware runs.

Troubleshooting

  • Policy shakes on the real robot — Add actuator/latency models and randomise them; low-pass filter actions.
  • Vision policy fails — Randomise textures/lighting more widely; use real images for fine-tuning.

Contrasted with

  • World Models — Simulators are hand-built physics engines; world models are learned simulators.
