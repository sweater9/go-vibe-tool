Overfitting and Regularization

conceptfreshness: stablesources not yet verified

Overfitting is when a model memorises training data and fails on new data. Regularisation, more data, early stopping and simpler models reduce it.

Short answer: If training performance is great but validation performance is poor, the model is overfitting. Remedies: more or better data, augmentation, regularisation (weight decay, dropout), early stopping, simpler models.

What is it?

A student who memorises past exam answers can ace those exams and fail new ones. A model that memorises its training data does the same. Regularisation is the set of tricks that discourage memorising and encourage learning general patterns.

Also known as: overfitting, underfitting, regularisation, regularization, dropout, weight decay, early stopping, bias variance tradeoff.

Technical definition

Overfitting reflects variance: low training error, high generalisation error. Diagnose with learning curves and validation metrics. Mitigations: more data, data augmentation, L2/weight decay, dropout (Srivastava et al., 2014), early stopping, model capacity reduction, ensembling, label smoothing, cross-validation for model selection. The opposite failure, underfitting, shows high error on both sets. Note that very large models can generalise well despite interpolating training data, so capacity alone does not determine overfitting.

Entity type: concept · Freshness class: stable.

When should I use it?

Always relevant when training; central to small-data work.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: Supervised Learning, Neural Networks.

Related concepts: Backpropagation and Gradient Descent, Transfer Learning, How to Evaluate AI Systems and Outputs, Deep Learning.

Implementation patterns

  • Learning curves: plot train vs validation metric over data size and epochs.
  • Early stopping on validation loss.
  • Augmentation for images/audio.
  • Cross-validation for model selection on small data.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: scikit-learn, PyTorch dropout, weight decay in AdamW, Albumentations (augmentation). Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Hold out validation and test sets properly.
  2. Plot learning curves.
  3. Add one regularisation measure at a time.
  4. Use the test set once at the end.
  5. Re-check after deployment (Model Drift and Production Monitoring).

Common failure modes

  • Tuning on the test set, overfitting to it indirectly.
  • Leakage that makes validation look good.
  • Too much regularisation causing underfitting.
  • Ignoring distribution shift between validation and real use.

Troubleshooting

  • Validation loss rising while training loss falls — Stop earlier, add regularisation or data.
  • Both losses high — Underfitting: increase capacity, train longer, improve features.

Contrasted with

  • Transfer Learning — Starting from pre-trained weights is itself a strong regulariser for small datasets.
