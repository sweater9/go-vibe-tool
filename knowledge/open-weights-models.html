Open-Weights vs Closed Models

conceptfreshness: volatilesources not yet verified

Open-weights models publish their trained parameters for download and use under a licence; closed models are available only through a provider's service. "Open weights" is not the same as "open source".

Short answer: Open weights: you can download and run/adapt the model under its licence. Closed: access via API only. Licences vary widely, and "open source AI" has its own definition (OSI) that many open-weight releases do not meet.

What is it?

Some AI models can be downloaded and run on your own machines — open-weights models. Others are only available through a company's service. Open weights give you control and privacy, but the licence terms differ and aren't always as free as the word "open" suggests.

Also known as: open weights, open weight models, open source LLM, open-source AI, Llama licence, open vs closed models, self-hosted models, open source AI definition.

Technical definition

Open-weights releases publish parameters (and sometimes code), but training data and recipes may be undisclosed and licences may add use restrictions (field-of-use, size thresholds, attribution). The Open Source Initiative published an Open Source AI Definition (v1.0, 2024) with requirements beyond weights availability; whether a given release qualifies must be checked against current texts. Trade-offs vs closed APIs: control, privacy, customisation and cost structure versus capability, convenience and maintenance burden.

Entity type: concept · Freshness class: volatile.

When should I use it?

Choose open weights when you need control, privacy or customisation; closed APIs when you need the strongest capability with minimal operations.

Implementation patterns

  • Read the licence and acceptable-use policy before adoption.
  • Pin model revisions by hash.
  • Keep a fallback hosted model.
  • Scan downloaded artefacts and prefer safe formats.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: Hugging Face Hub, Llama family, Mistral family, Qwen family, OSI Open Source AI Definition. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. List requirements (privacy, cost, customisation).
  2. Shortlist models; read licences.
  3. Verify provenance and checksums.
  4. Evaluate on your tasks.
  5. Plan serving (Model Serving and Inference Infrastructure).

Common failure modes

  • Assuming commercial use is allowed.
  • Downloading unverified weights or pickled files from unknown sources.
  • Ignoring that data provenance is undisclosed.
  • Underestimating operating cost.

Troubleshooting

  • Unsure about licence — Have legal review; record the licence version at download.
  • Supply-chain worry — Use safetensors, verify checksums, download from official organisations.
