Mechanistic Interpretability

techniquefreshness: evolvingsources not yet verified

Mechanistic interpretability tries to reverse-engineer what is happening inside neural networks — identifying features and circuits — rather than only observing inputs and outputs.

Short answer: It aims to explain model behaviour by analysing internal components (neurons, attention heads, features found with sparse autoencoders). Findings so far are partial and mostly on small or specific behaviours; it is a research area, not a turnkey audit tool.

What is it?

Instead of treating an AI as a black box, interpretability researchers open it up and try to find which internal parts do what — like tracing wires in a circuit. The aim is to understand, and eventually verify, why a model does what it does.

Also known as: interpretability, mech interp, explainable AI, XAI, sparse autoencoders, circuits, neuron analysis, activation patching.

Technical definition

Techniques include circuit analysis (Olah et al., "Zoom In", 2020; Elhage et al., 2021), probing classifiers, activation patching/causal interventions, and dictionary learning with sparse autoencoders to extract more interpretable features from activations (Cunningham et al., 2023; Anthropic "Towards Monosemanticity", 2023). Limitations: polysemantic neurons, scaling to large models, faithfulness of explanations, and gaps between interpreting a feature and guaranteeing safety. Generated chain-of-thought text is not a substitute for internal analysis (Reasoning Transparency and Hidden Reasoning).

Entity type: technique · Freshness class: evolving.

When should I use it?

Research and high-stakes auditing contexts; for most applications, rely on behavioural evaluation.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: Neural Networks, What is a Transformer? (Attention Explained).

Related concepts: AI Alignment, Reasoning Transparency and Hidden Reasoning, AI Governance, AI Bias and Fairness, Red-Teaming AI Systems.

Implementation patterns

  • Probe layers for encoded concepts.
  • Activation patching to find causally important components.
  • SAE feature dashboards for exploration.
  • Use SHAP/LIME-style attributions for tabular/classical models.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: TransformerLens, sparse autoencoder libraries, SHAP, LIME, Captum. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. State the behaviour to explain.
  2. Pick a method suited to the model type.
  3. Validate with interventions.
  4. Report limits.

Common failure modes

  • Over-interpreting correlational probes.
  • Cherry-picked examples.
  • Assuming interpretability results scale to frontier models.
  • Treating post-hoc attributions as ground truth.

Troubleshooting

  • Explanation does not predict behaviour — Test with interventions rather than correlations.
  • Attributions unstable — Use multiple methods and sanity checks.

Contrasted with

  • Reasoning Transparency and Hidden Reasoning — Generated reasoning is output text; interpretability inspects internal computation.
