LoRA vs Full Fine-Tuning

conceptfreshness: evolvingsources not yet verified

LoRA trains small adapters on a frozen model; full fine-tuning updates all weights. LoRA is cheaper and easier to manage; full fine-tuning can fit large shifts better.

Short answer: LoRA: low memory, small artefacts, swappable adapters, quality often close for many tasks. Full fine-tuning: highest capacity and potentially best on big domain shifts, but costly and heavier to store and serve. Start with LoRA and escalate only if evaluation shows a gap.

What is it?

Full fine-tuning rewrites the whole model; LoRA adds a small trainable layer on top. LoRA is far cheaper and lets you keep many variants; full tuning has more freedom to change the model.

Also known as: LoRA vs fine-tuning, full fine-tuning vs LoRA, PEFT vs full finetune, should I use LoRA, LoRA or full finetuning.

When should I use it?

Default to LoRA; escalate on measured need.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: LoRA and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT), What is Fine-Tuning an AI Model?.

Related concepts: Quantization of AI Models, Instruction Tuning.

Implementation patterns

  • LoRA first; compare against full fine-tune only if the gap matters.
  • Full fine-tune when you have the hardware and a large domain shift.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: Hugging Face PEFT, DeepSpeed, FSDP. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Estimate memory budget.
  2. Run LoRA baseline.
  3. Evaluate gap vs full tuning on a pilot.
  4. Decide based on quality, cost and serving.

Common failure modes

  • Paying for full fine-tuning without proving a quality gap.
  • Under-ranking LoRA and concluding PEFT does not work.

Troubleshooting

  • LoRA underfits — Raise rank, target more modules or train longer before switching.
