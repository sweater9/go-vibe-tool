Vision Transformers (ViT)

modelfreshness: evolvingsources not yet verified

Vision Transformers split an image into patches and process them with transformer attention, matching or beating CNNs at scale.

Short answer: ViT cuts an image into patches treated like tokens and runs a standard transformer. It needs large-scale pre-training to shine and underlies many multimodal models.

What is it?

Instead of sliding filters over an image, a Vision Transformer chops it into small squares ("patches"), treats each like a word, and lets attention work out how the patches relate. Given enough training data it performs very well and forms the visual half of many multimodal AI systems.

Also known as: ViT, vision transformer, image transformer, patch embedding, Swin transformer, DeiT, transformers for images.

Technical definition

ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020) linearly embeds fixed-size patches, adds position embeddings and a class token, and applies transformer encoder layers. Lacking CNN inductive biases, it generally needs large datasets or strong augmentation/distillation to match CNNs on smaller data. Variants: Swin (shifted windows), DeiT (data-efficient training), and use as image encoders in Contrastive Learning and CLIP and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Cost grows quadratically with patch count.

Entity type: model · Freshness class: evolving.

When should I use it?

Use with pre-trained weights for classification, detection and as visual encoders in multimodal systems.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: What is a Transformer? (Attention Explained), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs).

Related concepts: CNN vs Vision Transformer, Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Contrastive Learning and CLIP, What is Multimodal AI?, Transfer Learning.

Implementation patterns

  • Use a pre-trained ViT backbone with a task head.
  • Larger patch size to cut compute.
  • Hierarchical/windowed variants for dense tasks.
  • ViT as the image encoder in a VLM.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: Hugging Face Transformers, timm, PyTorch. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Pick a pre-trained checkpoint.
  2. Match input resolution/normalisation.
  3. Fine-tune with small LR and layer-wise decay.
  4. Compare against a CNN baseline.

Common failure modes

  • Overfitting when trained from scratch on small data.
  • High memory at high resolution.
  • Resolution mismatch with pre-training position embeddings.
  • Ignoring licence of pre-trained weights.

Troubleshooting

  • Poor accuracy on small dataset — Fine-tune a pre-trained ViT or use a CNN.
  • Out of memory — Lower resolution, larger patches, gradient checkpointing, or windowed variants.

Contrasted with

  • Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) — CNNs build in locality; ViT learns spatial relations from data.
