Knowledge Distillation

techniquefreshness: volatilesources not yet verified

Distillation trains a smaller student model to imitate a larger teacher, capturing much of its behaviour at lower cost.

Short answer: Distillation = train a small student on a big teacher's outputs (soft probabilities or generated data). It can give cheaper, faster models for narrow tasks; check teacher licence terms and expect a quality gap on hard cases.

What is it?

A large model is the teacher; a smaller model is the student that learns to copy the teacher's answers. The student ends up much cheaper to run while keeping much of the teacher's skill on the tasks it was taught.

Also known as: model distillation, teacher student, distilled model, DistilBERT, distill a large model, synthetic data from a bigger model.

Technical definition

Classic distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) minimises divergence between student and teacher output distributions, often with temperature softening; DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019) is a well-known example. For LLMs, "distillation" often means supervised training on teacher-generated text or reasoning traces. Provider terms of service may restrict using model outputs to train competing models — verify before doing so.

Entity type: technique · Freshness class: volatile.

When should I use it?

Use for high-volume narrow tasks where cost or latency matters.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: What is Fine-Tuning an AI Model?.

Related concepts: Small Language Models (SLMs), Quantization of AI Models, Instruction Tuning, LLM Cost Optimization, Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning (RLVR and GRPO).

Implementation patterns

  • Generate task-specific data with a teacher, filter, fine-tune a small model.
  • Logit distillation where teacher logits are available (open models).
  • Evaluate student vs teacher on your task.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: TRL, DistilBERT, synthetic data pipelines. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Check the teacher's licence/terms.
  2. Define the narrow task.
  3. Generate and filter data.
  4. Train and evaluate the student.
  5. Route hard cases to the teacher.

Common failure modes

  • Student inherits teacher errors and biases.
  • Terms-of-use violations.
  • Distribution gap between synthetic and real inputs.
  • Over-claiming equivalence with the teacher.

Troubleshooting

  • Student weak on hard cases — Add more hard examples; keep the teacher for escalation.

Contrasted with

  • Quantization of AI Models — Quantisation compresses numbers; distillation trains a different, smaller model.
  • What is Fine-Tuning an AI Model? — Distillation is fine-tuning with a teacher's outputs as the target.
