Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)

conceptfreshness: stablesources not yet verified

A VAE learns to compress data into a smooth probabilistic latent space and decode samples from it, enabling generation and representation learning.

Short answer: A VAE has an encoder to a latent distribution and a decoder back, trained with a reconstruction loss plus a KL regulariser. It gives structured latent spaces; latent diffusion uses VAE-style autoencoders for compression.

What is it?

A VAE squeezes data (like an image) into a short code, then learns to rebuild it. Because the codes are encouraged to form a smooth, well-behaved space, you can pick new codes and decode them into new, plausible samples.

Also known as: VAE, variational autoencoder, autoencoder, latent space, VQ-VAE, reparameterization trick, ELBO, beta-VAE.

Technical definition

Kingma & Welling (2013) maximise the evidence lower bound: expected reconstruction log-likelihood minus KL(q(z|x) || p(z)), using the reparameterisation trick for gradients. Outputs can be blurrier than GANs/diffusion; extensions include β-VAE (disentanglement) and VQ-VAE (discrete codes), the latter widely used as image/audio tokenisers.

Entity type: concept · Freshness class: stable.

When should I use it?

Useful for compression, representation learning, anomaly detection and as the autoencoder inside latent diffusion.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: Neural Networks, Unsupervised Learning.

Related concepts: Diffusion Models, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), What are Embeddings in AI?, What is Multimodal AI?.

Implementation patterns

  • Latent compression before diffusion.
  • VQ-VAE tokenisation for images/audio.
  • Anomaly detection by reconstruction error.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: PyTorch, VQ-VAE tokenisers, Stable Diffusion's autoencoder. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Choose continuous vs discrete latent.
  2. Tune KL weight.
  3. Visualise reconstructions and samples.
  4. Use as a component, e.g. for latent diffusion.

Common failure modes

  • Posterior collapse, where the decoder ignores the latent code.
  • Blurry reconstructions from the pixel-wise likelihood objective.
  • KL weight poorly tuned, trading reconstruction quality against latent regularity.

Troubleshooting

  • Decoder ignores latent — Anneal KL weight (KL warm-up) or adjust architecture.
  • Blurry outputs — Add perceptual/adversarial losses or use diffusion.

Contrasted with

  • Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) — GANs use a discriminator; VAEs optimise a likelihood bound.
  • Diffusion Models — Diffusion refines noise iteratively; VAEs sample a latent and decode once.
