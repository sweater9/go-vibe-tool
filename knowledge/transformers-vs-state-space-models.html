Transformers vs State Space Models

conceptfreshness: volatilesources not yet verified

Transformers keep full access to the token history through attention; state space models keep a compressed state. The trade-off is exact recall versus linear-time efficiency.

Short answer: Transformers: strongest ecosystem, precise in-context recall, quadratic attention cost and growing KV cache. SSMs: linear time, constant-size state, efficient long sequences, weaker exact recall. Hybrids try to get both. Verify with benchmarks on your task.

What is it?

Transformers can look back at any earlier word directly, which makes them precise but costly for very long inputs. State-space models carry a compact summary forward, which is cheaper but can lose details. Mixed designs combine them.

Also known as: transformer vs mamba, attention vs SSM, mamba vs transformer, transformer alternatives, are transformers going away.

When should I use it?

Use transformers by default; evaluate SSMs for long-sequence efficiency.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: What is a Transformer? (Attention Explained), State Space Models (Mamba and S4).

Related concepts: KV Cache, FlashAttention, What is a Context Window?.

Implementation patterns

  • Default to transformers for ecosystem; test SSM/hybrids when sequence length or memory dominate.
  • Evaluate retrieval/copy tasks explicitly.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: Mamba, FlashAttention, hybrid models. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Define sequence lengths.
  2. List candidate models.
  3. Benchmark quality + cost.
  4. Verify serving support.

Common failure modes

  • Choosing on headline throughput without checking quality on your tasks.
  • Ignoring tooling support.

Troubleshooting

  • Unsure — Run both on your longest realistic inputs and compare quality, latency, memory.
