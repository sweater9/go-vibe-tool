AI Alignment

conceptfreshness: evolvingsources not yet verified

AI alignment is the effort to make AI systems pursue the goals and values their developers and users actually intend — helpful, honest and safe — including when instructions are imperfect or situations are new.

Short answer: Alignment covers specifying what we want, training models toward it, and checking they do it — through techniques such as instruction tuning, preference optimisation (RLHF, DPO), constitutional methods, red-teaming and interpretability. It is unsolved; practical alignment today is risk reduction, not a guarantee.

What is it?

A powerful AI that does exactly what you literally said, or what looked rewarding during training, isn't necessarily doing what you meant. Alignment is the research and engineering work to close that gap — so models are helpful, don't deceive and avoid harmful behaviour.

Also known as: alignment, AI safety, value alignment, aligned AI, outer alignment, inner alignment, scalable oversight, helpful honest harmless.

Technical definition

Problem framings include outer alignment (is the specified objective what we want?), inner alignment (does the learned system pursue that objective?), reward misspecification and gaming (Reward Hacking and Over-Optimization), scalable oversight (supervising systems that exceed evaluator ability), robustness and honesty. Methods: Instruction Tuning, RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), Preference Optimization (Aligning Models from Human or AI Preferences) (including Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)), Constitutional AI and RLAIF (AI Feedback), adversarial testing (Red-Teaming AI Systems), and Mechanistic Interpretability. Surveys such as Amodei et al. (2016) and the RLHF limitations paper (Casper et al., 2023) document open problems. Claims about long-term risk are contested and outside this entry's scope.

Entity type: concept · Freshness class: evolving.

When should I use it?

Relevant to anyone training, fine-tuning or deploying models, at least as a risk checklist.

Implementation patterns

  • Layer defences: aligned model + guardrails + permissions + monitoring.
  • Evaluate refusals, over-refusals and honesty on dedicated test sets.
  • Red-team before release and continuously.
  • Document limits in model cards.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: RLHF pipelines, DPO and variants, Constitutional AI, red-teaming programmes, interpretability tooling. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Define intended behaviour in writing.
  2. Build evaluation sets for helpfulness, honesty, harm and over-refusal.
  3. Choose training approach.
  4. Red-team.
  5. Monitor in production and iterate.

Common failure modes

  • Treating alignment as a one-time training step.
  • Over-refusal making the model unhelpful.
  • Sycophancy mistaken for helpfulness.
  • Reward models exploited during optimisation.
  • Evaluating only on known attack patterns.

Troubleshooting

  • Model agrees with false user claims — Add sycophancy tests; train on disagreement-appropriate data; evaluate honesty.
  • Too many refusals — Measure over-refusal rates and rebalance preference data.

Contrasted with

  • AI Guardrails — Guardrails are runtime checks around a model; alignment shapes the model's own behaviour.
  • AI Governance — Governance is organisational and legal oversight; alignment is a technical property and research field.

Related concepts

ConceptPreference Optimization (Aligning Models from Human or AI Preferences)Preference optimisation is the family of methods that tune a model using comparisons of better versus worse responses — RLHF, DPO and related algorithms. It is an alignment technique family, distinct from ordinary reinforcement learning.ConceptRLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)RLHF trains a language model to behave in ways people prefer: gather human comparisons of outputs, fit a reward model, then optimise the model against that reward with reinforcement learning under a KL constraint.ConceptDirect Preference Optimization (DPO)DPO aligns a language model directly from preference pairs using a simple classification-style loss against a reference model — without training a separate reward model or running an online RL loop.ConceptConstitutional AI and RLAIF (AI Feedback)Constitutional AI trains a model to critique and revise its own outputs against a written set of principles, and uses AI-generated preference labels (RLAIF) instead of or alongside human ones.ConceptReward Hacking and Over-OptimizationReward hacking happens when an AI system exploits flaws in its reward signal — scoring high without doing what was intended — including over-optimising a learned reward model.ConceptSycophancy in Language ModelsSycophancy is a model's tendency to tell users what they want to hear — agreeing with mistaken claims or flattering them — rather than what is accurate.ConceptRed-Teaming AI SystemsRed-teaming is structured adversarial testing of an AI system — humans or models trying to make it fail, misbehave or leak — to find weaknesses before real attackers or users do.ConceptMechanistic InterpretabilityMechanistic interpretability tries to reverse-engineer what is happening inside neural networks — identifying features and circuits — rather than only observing inputs and outputs.ConceptAI GovernanceAI governance is the set of policies, roles, processes and legal controls an organisation or government uses to ensure AI is developed and used responsibly, safely and lawfully.ConceptWhat is a Large Language Model (LLM)?A large language model is a neural network trained on vast amounts of text to predict the next token, which lets it write, summarise, translate, reason about text and write code.
