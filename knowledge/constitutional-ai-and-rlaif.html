Constitutional AI and RLAIF (AI Feedback)

techniquefreshness: evolvingsources not yet verified

Constitutional AI trains a model to critique and revise its own outputs against a written set of principles, and uses AI-generated preference labels (RLAIF) instead of or alongside human ones.

Short answer: A model critiques and revises answers using written principles, and AI-labelled preferences then drive training (RLAIF). It reduces human labelling needs and makes values more explicit; it still depends on the labeller model and chosen principles.

What is it?

Instead of people judging every answer, a short written "constitution" of principles is used: the AI critiques its own draft against them, rewrites it, and AI-made comparisons are used to train the model further. The rules become visible and editable, though they are only as good as the principles and the AI applying them.

Also known as: Constitutional AI, CAI, RLAIF, reinforcement learning from AI feedback, AI feedback, self-critique, model constitution, principle-based alignment.

Technical definition

Bai et al. (2022) describe a supervised phase (self-critique and revision against sampled principles, then fine-tuning on revisions) and an RL phase using AI-generated preference labels (RL from AI Feedback). Lee et al. (2023) compare RLAIF with RLHF on summarisation/dialogue tasks. Open issues: labeller bias, principle selection and conflicts, and evaluation of whether behaviour truly matches stated principles. Related practice: deliberative approaches and spec-style documents describing intended behaviour.

Entity type: technique · Freshness class: evolving.

When should I use it?

Useful where human labelling is costly or principles must be explicit.

Prerequisites and related concepts

Understand these first: RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), Preference Optimization (Aligning Models from Human or AI Preferences).

Related concepts: AI Alignment, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), LLM-as-a-Judge, Red-Teaming AI Systems, AI Guardrails, Sycophancy in Language Models.

Implementation patterns

  • Write principles; sample critique prompts; revise; fine-tune.
  • Generate AI preference pairs; train with DPO/RL.
  • Human-audit samples of AI labels.
  • Version the principle set.

Technologies and frameworks

Commonly associated: principle-driven critique/revision prompts, preference-pair generation with a labeller model. Names are examples of what exists, not recommendations, and projects change quickly.

Practical guide

  1. Draft principles.
  2. Test critique/revision quality manually.
  3. Generate data at scale.
  4. Audit with humans.
  5. Evaluate behaviour on held-out scenarios.

Common failure modes

  • Labeller model bias amplified in training.
  • Vague or conflicting principles.
  • Evasive/over-refusing behaviour.
  • No human validation of AI labels.

Troubleshooting

  • Model over-refuses — Add helpfulness principles and measure over-refusal.
  • AI labels disagree with humans — Calibrate on a human-labelled subset; refine prompts.

Contrasted with

  • RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) — RLHF uses human preference labels; RLAIF uses model-generated labels guided by principles.
  • AI Guardrails — Guardrails check outputs at runtime; constitutional methods change training.
